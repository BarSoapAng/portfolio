周誉晓 (zhōu yù xiǎo).
That’s my Chinese name.

I came to Canada when I was 7, airdropped into my relative’s home without knowing English, or really much of anything about the world. Although I’ve spent 12 years (most of my life) in Canada, I love being Chinese. I love the food, the culture, the people, and my home.

This week, you and me, let’s celebrate what it means to be Chinese.

Let’s eat dumplings and 年糕 (nián gāo).
Let’s open red packets.
Let’s hop on WeChat for the first time in forever.
Let’s speak Chinese to our families, even if it comes out awkward and broken.

My parents sacrificed a lot for me to be here. I’m sure yours did too.

And regardless of how life turned out, the good and the bad, let’s thank them for another year of being alive.

There are parts of their identity, and parts of yours, that were given up to be Canadian. I’m sure you feel it too. And whether that’s good or bad, let’s be thankful for who we’ve become.

**You are lovely. you are beautiful.** Whoever you are and whoever you wish to be, you are uniquely you.

And on LNY, while I sit alone, eating pork dumplings next to scattered midterm notes, I will look out for you, and I will be proud of you.

**Love,**
Angela
