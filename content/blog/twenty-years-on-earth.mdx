officially no longer a teenager, so here’s the things I learned in my 20 years on Earth:

no ai, only my hand typed slop :P

Embracing the struggle is better than celebrating the win.
I stood over my kitchen counter, 1am, sobbing. I received 2 job rejections that week, both of which I put in a lot of effort preparing (one I spent a week on the take-home LOL). I remember the misery and dread I felt that day. And I remember thinking, with tears falling down my face, that this would be such an interesting moment a month or two from now. to struggle and to feel deeply dissatisfied is so genuinely beautiful. Because it was then I felt that the cost of what I consider success was paid in full.

Great things take time.
maybe that’s living a slower life, maybe thats reading your code line by line. There’s a lot that’s shaped my thoughts on this recently, but it’s mostly been polarizing. We’re always in this constant race against time, against each other, and I guess now against AI. There’s someone who’s learning and shipping faster than you and suddenly the golden metric is speed and not quality. Man, back in high school I spend a whole day writing a goddamn gameloop that let you move a square with arrow keys, and now people be deploying whole websites in minutes. but god am I thankful for those good old slow AI-less days

people’s perception of you is so fake.
i’ve never been so uncomfortable in my skin (or just hair ig) in my life. it’s been easy to say I love myself when there was nothing at stake. for the first time, I finally challenged MY notion of confidence, and it’s TERRIFYING! every day, I have to re-establish my perception of self, aside from what I perceive others to perceive me to be. and you know what? the fake it till you make things really does work, and you might find that 95% of it is just in your head :)

You never know yourself.
it’s ok to not know every part of yourself. in fact, i’d argue this whole self-understanding thing is a paradox. sure you could reason and deduce your decisions, but it’s so much more nuanced than that. i feel like the moment you become sure of your personally curated identity is also the moment it is broken. so I think it’s less about knowing who you are, today, but about accepting the you that’ll be different tomorrow

for the tweens and teens out there, please, live a carefree life of joy and purpose. the cannon events in your life will make you a better, stronger person, even if they suck in the moment. i promise, and you should believe this too, that there’s nothing you can’t do!

what a beautiful era to be alive &lt;3
