In September 2026, I was in one of our regular standups for oro, when I was made aware of Stan Launchpad.

For some context, oro is the startup I joined pre-product in my 2A at Waterloo. I met Sunny, co-founder, through her posts on LinkedIn about working on oro. The idea inspired me so I reached out, we grabbed coffee, and I joined as a founding eng.

And by the 3rd month I was working across product and ops. Finally by September, I became the co-founder along with Sunny.

We found out about Stan Launchpad and Sunny brought up the concept of joining a reality TV show where you get 2 weeks to scale your startup. This was genuinely such a crazy concept and it made me quite intimidated to be honest LOL

And many many times over the next week I would think about applying. There were thoughts I kept going back to over and over again:
- I wouldn't fit into the community
- I would look hella goofy on camera

So many many times I would consider backing out of applying.

## Applying

Finally the week of the application deadline, I applied with Sunny. I remember filming our video application at the Tesla office that day. It was 9pm and I was once again alone in the office. We spent a good 2 hours perfecting that video (although it was far from perfect haha)

After a full week of waiting, we finally got the news that WE WERE IN!!!

## Prep

The 2 weeks before launchpad were lowkey some of the most brutal work days I've ever had. I would:
- wake up at 8-9 am every day and go to work
- do Tesla work until 5-6pm
- skip lunchtime to work on oro
- eat dinner at the office
- work on oro until 1am
- go home, work on content, sleep, repeat

I genuinely lived and breathed code every single day. I would take the weekend to maybe go on a hike or explore the city but that's it.

I re-started making content in preparation, and I genuinely forgot how much time the whole process takes LMAO

My manager was lowkey so chill and supportive of the whole thing. And to be honest, I do own a part of my success here to him as well haha

---

Throughout the weeks leading up to it, we had a few meetings scheduled with all the contestants. It was there we met Max from Hivemind Research. He declared alliances and even sent us a video with captions introducing himself. What an absolute menace :)

We also had some interviews with Tien, the producer, and Keegan, a skitmaster on the Beast team.

## Day 1

Waking up at 7 AM on Sunday (Oct 4th), we rushed out of our airbnb with massive luggages to arrive at the pickup spot. Sunny and I wore our new oro hoodies loud and proud! 

It was easy to spot the crew, with 100 founders and even more team members crowding around a few coach buses. Did the usual logistics: checked in, met our camp counsellor, talked to some other founders, etc...

We hopped on the bus, hoping to get some work done before the chaos started. We were able to! Spoke to Vitoria, founder of Linked30 (she has an incredible story btw), and mingled with a few other competitors. Our cabin-mate, Paige, sat behind us with her teammate Ying.

10 minutes away from arriving, the bus errupted into a party. Jonathan, another counsellor, pulled out someone's speaker and started blasting music. Amacio, a Spanish team of 2, bursted out in songs, pulling out their drums and really brought up the vibes. We partied the whole way to the camp site.

The camera crew crowded around the bus, we volunteered to film a hype segment with them. Our arrival was full of hype, yelling and screaming as we passed by cameras, showing off our brand and our vibes. When we got our cabins, it turned out that some miscommunication put me in a private cabin, away from Sunny. But after some discussions, I ended up getting placed back in there with them. There were a good 10 beds on either sides of the cabin. Sunny and I lived on one side, while Paige lived on the other, connected by a long washroom section. On our beds we found our purple Stan merch, a nice water bottle, and our name plaques. We barely had time to settle before putting on our new hoodies (that we would wear almost every single day LOL). It was time for our first meeting at the Barn, a building we would spend every morning at for the next 14 days.

## Day 2

The first challenge was to create 3 pieces of content, 1 of which (short form) will be judged by John himself. Completion was worth 75 points, and the singular video would be graded on another 75 point scale. Submission due at midnight

We decided to submit 2 LinkedIn posts first. Got that out of the way by 12 PM.

John came by to our table, where we asked him for some feedback on our content. He gave us really good advice about formats and what was wrong with our content. He seemed to have great expecations for us.

When it came to the founder led content, Sunny decided to take that on. She scripted and filmed a whole video with the 2 of us, before scraping that (for the challenge) and re-scripting and editing a whole new video. We had standup with the team that night, which took an hour. So it was 11:30PM. And she was roughly halfway done editing. I remember sitting there with the submission form filled in, waiting for her final piece of content.

It was then 11:45PM. I began urging her to finish up as much as possible and to just at least send it.

Then it was 11:50. The ending was barely finished, I told her to just use stock videos instead of b-roll to get that just done.

11:55PM. She starts exporting

11:57PM. She uploads it to Instagram

11:58PM. We pull up the exact time on google and stare at it

The next 2 minutes were some of the longest I had ever experienced. The Instagram upload bar refused to budge past 90% and things started looking a little cooked. I took her phone outdoors hoping there would be just slightly better signal.

11:59:00PM. 

11:59:20PM Instagram finally uploaded. I ran back indoors, refreshed my page and submitted.

11:59:35PM was the submission time.

## Day 3

Ok like I feel like I don't need to yap too much about this day. Gist is that Sunny felt really down about the video. John absolutely fucking roasted us in front of every single founder. I ran to the washroom and came back to find Sunny crying behind the cabin with Alexa. The producers called us for an interview and Sunny was crying. They asked me "how does it feel to see your cofounder like this?" so I also started crying. And we were literally just bawling with cameras in our faces.

Anyways, this was also the first elimination night!!! 10 teams were going to be eliminated

We gathered around a bonfire, where John and Vitali revealed the names of eliminated teams one-by-one. John started off the session by asking us "oro team, how are you feeling." To which we both chuckled a little and responded "much better haha".

Eliminated teams stood in front of John and Vitali on a red carpet, where they would say every single thing that was wrong with the video. Vitali would then hand the team their wooden plaque, for them to throw it into the fire.

One team, MyMark (a solo team Rey), refused to give up. He bargained with John and Vitali before finally leaving his plaque before the fire. He urged the duo to reconsider his elimination, which Vitali swiftly declined by tossing his plaque into the fire anyways.

As Vitali moved up the leaderboard from the bottom, my heart skipped a beat. Every time he would turn a plaque, I felt my heart drop. My hand clasped onto Sunnys, the cold almost didn't seem to matter anymore. And with every turn, I would let out a breath of relief.

Finally, it turned out we were 18th. 

## Day 4-9

These days were not as interesting. Mostly just completing the challenges.

We would get points for:
- social media posts (max 5/day)
- customer interviews (max 10)
- sales from customer calls (max 10)

And milestones gave massive points:
- public launch
- 3 new features built and posted about
- first 3 sales
- 10 customer interviews completed

We went down every single person on our waitlist and messaged them. We managed to schedule around 40 calls in those few days.

On Day 4, we had not done a singular sales call prior to Launchpad. We jumped into the whole thing blind. We gave discounts, we begged. And at the end of it all, we closed 1/10 calls.

The next day, we asked Vitali for help. In just 15 minutes, one piece of advice, our close rate for the next 3 days would jump to 90%.

## Day 10

I experienced one of the most stressful things in my life here, and there's not many things that stress me out this much LMAO

Unfortunately we entered an NDA regarding this with Stan so you won't be hearing about it :3

## Day 11

They switched to eliminations via revenue. Every dollar was worth a point. This was pretty fucked because we were B2C, and not even launched.

Our next priority was to launch. I stayed up all night for this launch. Spent like 4 hours making our launch video which was motion graphics, which I'd never done before. It actually did pretty well LMAO. writing our launch posts

I set up our product hunt for the first time and scheduled it. Turns out i missed the 3AM deadline for the day so it had to launch a day later on product hunt

We messaged literally every single person we knew begging them to comment and engage with our LinkedIn launch post. And it did really relaly well because of that.

Sunny and I spent a huge chunk of time fixing up our landing again, fixing onboarding, the paywall. It was a crazy rush.

## Day 12

Morning of the launch, our onboarding was still not ready. We scheduled launch at 10:30AM, told all of our friends and family the day before.

I was sooooo flipping stressed man. Our deploy pipeline takes ~10min to run, which is crazy IK, and watching the clock tick down.

At 10:30AM, we were still not ready. The user states from onboarding just was just not working out, it was pretty entangled with our previous waitlisting structure.

It finally worked at 11:30AM.

We then spent the next hour messaging everyone again with our launch links. Skipped lunch

It was 5PM. We realized that we were NOT going to win, or even get close to being elimination safe, from just promoting our product. We needed another way to get revenue.

Inspired by Memoe (Jia and Angie) who were running ad spaces and selling their domain names, Sunny and I made a decision to pivot.

No more oro promotion, we became an ad merch agency.

At 7PM, I started coding up a page on our website to support merch ad space bidding. Sunny started contacting friends to buy some ad space. She ended up getting a mayoral candidate for Waterloo, Jerry, to buy a few blocks

At 10PM, the site was done. It was scrappy, sure, and was barely functional, but done nonetheless.

Sunny sent out reels, posts, everything on social media. She filmed like 4 reels in one sitting that night. I was fixing up the non-functional site while she began promoting.

## Day 13

One of my most stressful mornings. 

Orders came in and people wanted their images on our hoodie. So I actually didn't support putting in images through the website, so I was manaully making graphics for these people, deploying them on our sit, and then running SQL queries directly in our database to update the slots that people purchased.

One of the fun parts about our ad space merch was that you can outbid other people. So a grid starts at $10 but you can purchase it off someone else for $11. To buy it back you'd pay the difference.

2 people REALLLLLY went at it.

It was awesome seeing them so invested, but it was also hella hard to keep up with all of their updates hahaha

The submission deadline for revenue was 2 PM. We ended up making ~$1,000 in those few hours

But uh anyways we were eliminated :3

Actually didn't feel any sadness. I felt very at ease with this decision because geuinely I dont know how much harder it was possible to work for this

When we walked around the fire to get to our seats, everyone would cheer for your team the entire way there. That was real family. We were no longer competing with each other, we were friends supporting every one in everything we do. That was an incredibbly incredibly beautiful moment

## Day 14 - end

Yeah we lowkey slacked off for the rest of the days. We worked on our pitch deck (which still needs A LOT of work) and practiced our pitches for the first time. Sunny and I went tubing. I tried waterskiing and ate shit. I yapped with Daniel Kim, an absolutely goated creator. And I ate good, did not sleep good.

## End

I think the story back in NYC is not as interesting. We went to eat ramen and I got some NYC pizza before flying out. 

These people were absolutely amazing. I've written over 230 lines of markdown up till this point but I'd still not be able to convey the ambience and emotions I felt there. To be a part of such an AMAZING group of people at such a beautiful campsite was such a blessing. There was never short of stuff to do and every day felt like a whole year of learning. I never expected to grow so so much in such a short amount of time, but here I am. Launchpad showed me the sky's the limit, and if you can dream it, you can do it.

So so so much love goes out to John and Vitali for making this show a reality. And so so so much love to every single person, who supported us along the way, especially when we were down.

You guys are the best. I'll read back at this maybe 10 years in the future and relive these moments.
